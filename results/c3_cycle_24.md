## The headline: a projection that is a measurement

`prime-predicate` enters the ledger with **five subjects**, which makes it the
largest of the new groups on its first cycle.  What makes it worth more than
its count is that the refill it promises was **measured, not projected.**

Every one of 132–136 is a Goldbach verification statement, whose faithful
reading is a bounded ∀ over an ∃ pair (or triple) of primes.  Each was re-run
with an **admitted unary predicate of the same arity** (`even` / `odd`)
substituted for `prime` and **nothing else changed** — and **all five
CERTIFY**, including 132 at its full `4 × 10^18` bound.

So the fragment already owns every other thing these subjects need: the
∀-outer/∃-inner shape, the bounded hypothesis, the arithmetic, the literal
bound at any magnitude.  A decidable bounded-primality predicate returns five
subjects to ready and **no second blocker is waiting behind it** — which is the
cycle-05 re-wedge foreclosed by evidence rather than by argument.  Declaring a
row for it is a **purchase-axis call**, not this loop's.

Two independent instruments now agree on this demand, having reached it by
different routes.  The **lexical census** carries `primality` as a standing
miss signal over **170 nodes** and has since the portfolio was first taken; it
reads words.  **Certification** has now measured five subjects refusing on
exactly that primitive; it reads the gate.  The census said where to look and
the gate said what it costs.

`prime-predicate` is filed apart from `defined-predicate` on purpose.  That
signal names a predicate the *corpus* defines (`superpowered`, cycle 12), which
a definitional-extension purchase retires.  `prime` is a standing mathematical
predicate that no source here defines, so P8-shaped machinery would **not**
return these subjects, and one signal covering both would promise a purchase
that does not exist.

## The caveat that belongs beside it, stated rather than implied

The eval box is **radius 8**.  So a certified reading of *"the even Goldbach
conjecture is verified up to 4 × 10^18"* would be evidence about **n ≤ 8**, and
the stated bound — the entire distinguishing content of the sentence — would
sit inert outside the box.  The `even`-substituted probes above certify for
precisely that reason: at 4 × 10^18 and at 30 they are the *same* computation.

This is recorded as a **receipt finding and not a sixth signal**, because a
signal is a promise about which purchase returns a subject and no purchase is
promised by "the box is smaller than the claim".  It is real demand for a
deeper instance box, and it is the cycle-15/17/18 shallow-teeth pattern
appearing at a new scale: there, a green was partly coverage; here, a green
would be *almost entirely* coverage.  A future cycle that buys `prime` and
ships these five should say so in the same breath.

## 131: the fragment can say it and the mirrors cannot check it

`div-remainder` is the one subject whose refusal is not a missing primitive.
Its faithful reading — six outer parameters and a two-variable ∃ — is the
**supported** ∀\*∃\* shape, and it is the eval mirror's combinatorial ceiling
that stops it: 6,975,757,441 conclusion evaluations against a 2,000,000 ceiling.
The gate honest-skips rather than hang, which is the right behaviour and worth
naming as its own signal (`exists-domain-too-large`) because what it prices is
a **witness search that beats exhaustive enumeration**, never a new node class.

Dropping the reading to two outer parameters puts it under the ceiling, and
there it is **REFUTED**, correctly, at `p1 = 0`: `0 < r < 4·p₁p₂p₃` is
unsatisfiable when the product is zero.  The node's truth depends on the
blueprint's ambient hypothesis that the p's are primes — and that hypothesis
lives in the surrounding chapter, not in the extracted node.

That is a **second, distinct finding about the corpus rather than the
fragment**, and it is filed as `elided-ambient-hypothesis`.  It is not
`metatheoretic-subject` (cycle 14/23), which names a node carrying *no*
statement; this names a node carrying a statement that is **not
self-contained**.  No purchase retires it: what it prices is an extractor that
carries a node's ambient hypotheses with it, which is corpus-intake work.
Cycle 23 found that a lexical census cannot tell a theorem from a proof remark.
This is the neighbouring finding — it cannot tell a self-contained theorem from
a chapter-local one either.

## 137: the fragment can count a filtered set and cannot sum one

`highlyabundant-def` refuses three times, each isolated so that none of the
three masks the others.

1. `unknown term operator 'sigma'` — the ordinary `function-symbol` reading.
2. Unfolding σ to the source's **own written definition** ("the sum of the

   P1 bought literal-bound iteration, P2 bought the literal-bound set, and both
   stop in the same place.  Filed apart, because retiring the `setbuild` bound
   does not retire the `bigsum` bound — separate checks in separate validators.
3. Even at a literal bound, σ sums the **divisors**, and `bigsum takes exactly

The control that makes (3) a reading rather than an inference: `card` over the
**same** filtered set (`setbuild` with a `dvd` filter at literal bounds)
**certifies**.  So the fragment can COUNT a filtered set and cannot SUM one,
and `filtered-bigop` prices the sum-over-setbuild rung on machinery **P2
already bought** — the cheapest-looking of the new rows.

## Addendum: the cycle could not self-merge, and why that is a real finding

This cycle shipped green and then **could not merge itself**, for a reason that
had nothing to do with its content — and the SELF-MERGE rule was **right** to
refuse.

The ship commit was pushed while the claim PR was still a **draft**, and the PR
was marked ready afterwards.  `ready_for_review` is not one of the default
`pull_request` event types (opened / synchronize / reopened), so **no
`pull_request` event ever fired for the ship sha.**  Measured on PR #188:

| workflow | event | head sha | conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| `trust-surface` | `pull_request` | `e531d7d` (the empty **claim**) | success |
| `regression` | `push` | `0f7af87` (the **ship** commit) | skipped |
| `lean-hammer` | `push` | `0f7af87` | skipped |

`regression`'s jobs skip because `fast` is gated `if: github.event_name !=
'push' || github.ref == 'refs/heads/main'`, so a branch push runs nothing.  The
result is a head commit carrying **no `trust-surface`, no `fast`, no shards** —
and "trust-surface missing" is precisely the state the self-merge rule refuses
to merge on.  A green cycle, an empty check list, and a correct refusal.

This is the exact sibling of the purchase loop's already-recorded
*retitle-before-you-push* lesson: **on this repo CI is keyed to the state the PR
was in at push time, and no later edit re-keys it.**  Both driver prompts now
say to mark READY FOR REVIEW **before** pushing the ship commit, and
`C3_PROMPTS.md`'s Architecture section records the second way a trust-surface
check can go missing (previously documented as meaning exactly one thing: an
altered CI config).

Because prose about CI triggers is the thing `tests/test_trust_surface_fences.py`
exists to replace, the lesson is pinned as **three teeth, each mutation-verified**:
`trust-surface` must not enumerate `types:` (adding `ready_for_review` would
remove the hazard the prompts describe, so the tooth reds and the prompt gets
corrected in the same commit); the `fast` gate is pinned as its literal
condition; and both driver prompts must still carry the ordering instruction.

The corrective action was the one the amended prompt now prescribes — **push one
more real commit** so a fresh `synchronize` re-keys the checks, rather than
improvising around a missing fence.  That commit is this addendum, the prompt
fix, and the teeth.
